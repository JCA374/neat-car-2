PPO: one network, trained by trial and error

PPO keeps one fixed-shape neural network (two hidden layers of 256) and nudges its weights after every batch of driving, so choices that led to more reward become more likely. Eight copies of the game run in parallel on the CPU.

For eleven steps PPO barely drove. Putting it on the same rules as NEAT (p10) made that honest, and a quick lab found the fix: decide every 4 frames, not every frame. Since p14 PPO finishes most race tracks, and p15 all of them on both seeds.

Each card is one step. The numbers come from the same five benchmark tracks as every other model, with deltas against the step it builds on.

    The PPO lab: what made it work

    Short runs (600k frames, 5–10 minutes each), three seeds per recipe, scored only on the five validation tracks, never on the race tracks. Each row of squares is one seed: how many validation tracks its best lap check finished, and how many it still finished at the end of the run. Every recipe uses p11's settings and 16 forward rays.

    Learning against the clock

    Validation tracks finished at each lap check during training (average of the last five checks), against hours of wall-clock training. Steps from p10 on, the ones under the shared rules. The ring marks the checkpoint each step kept.
