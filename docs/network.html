Inside the evolved driver

Nobody designed this network. It started as 40 sensor inputs wired straight to 3 controls, and evolution added hidden nodes, rewired connections and tuned weights until it could lap a track it had never seen. This page shows the network itself, what it computes while driving, and how its ancestors drove along the way.

Reading the diagrams: inputs on the left (8 car readings, then 32 lidar distances labelled by direction: 0° is straight ahead, +90° is the car's left), evolved hidden nodes in the middle, and the three controls on the right. A blue connection pushes the next node up and a red one pushes it down. Thicker lines mean stronger weights. Hover over anything for details.

Look closely at the rays: they fan out around the car's right-hand side, which leaves a 36° blind gap centred on its left. That is how the sensor was built from day one. The docs had always called the rays "forward-biased", and only these drawings showed otherwise. Evolution learned to drive with it anyway.

3 · How the drivers evolved

Champions from milestone generations. Generation 0 is a random network. The others are the first champions to pass 300 and 600 on the validation tracks, and the best of each run. All five drive the same five benchmark tracks. None of these tracks was used for training or for picking the best genomes, so every drive is on an unseen course.
